AI Project · Build Workflow

Kuala Sepetang Tour Boat

Planning six fixed departures a day on one boat, when the weather decides how many of them run.

Type C · Career Path Builder Slug kuala-sepetang-tour-boat Started 21 Aug 2026 Lifts LLM APIs 4 · Prompt Eng 3 · Agents 7

The recurring decision this settles

Which of today's six departures do we run, which do we move, and how far ahead can we promise a slot to a customer?

One boat, six fixed two-hour departures at 09:00, 11:00, 13:00, 15:00, 17:00 and 19:00. With a single hull there is no rescheduling around a bad slot: a slot lost is a fare lost, and a slot run badly is twelve wet passengers a long way from the jetty. The operator already makes this call every morning off a phone weather app that reports the day rather than the window.

The build runs foundation-up. Each layer stands before the next one starts, so at every point there is something whole and load-bearing. One thing differs from a normal analytics build: the brain here is a classifier over a two-hour window, not a forecast. The forecast is bought in. What the project actually produces is a repeatable reading of that forecast against the operator's own thresholds, so the first thing proven is the classifier, not the weather.

The four layers

1
Forecast layer. One cached Open-Meteo call per date, timezone-pinned to Asia/Kuala_Lumpur. Hourly precipitation probability, rain, gusts, cloud, visibility, weather code and is_day, plus daily sunrise and sunset. No key, no account.
2
Rules layer. The operator's thresholds, the twelve activities with their windows, and the list of sheltered stops. One editable config file. This is the known truth and it belongs to the operator, not the model.
3
Decision layer. Per slot: rain shape, hazard versus comfort, activity fit, and a confidence tier set by lead time. Deterministic. Same forecast in, same verdict out.
4
Conversation layer. Multi-turn chat over four tools: live boat conditions, web search, a text editor that drafts the reply the operator sends, and the current date and time the other three depend on.

🌱 Plant the seeds · the roots

The forecast, and the rules it gets read against

1 Forecast layer: one cached call per date Done

A thin Open-Meteo client. One request per date, cached, serving every downstream function so a busy conversation does not make twenty calls. Free tier, no API key, no card.
Verify before writing the tool description
Timezone. Open-Meteo defaults to GMT. Pass timezone=Asia/Kuala_Lumpur or every hour is eight out and nothing looks broken. Resolution. minutely_15 coverage is strongest over Europe and North America; confirm what is available at 4.84N 100.63E before promising fine-grained rain shape. Variables. Confirm visibility and cape exist for the model selected here rather than assuming.
Template lesson
Check what the API actually returns at your coordinates before you write the schema. A tool description that promises a field the endpoint does not carry produces a failure the model cannot recover from.

2 Rules layer: thresholds, activities, shelter Done

Three tables in one editable config. They are the operator's judgment written down, not safety standards, and they are wrong until the operator corrects them. Keeping them in the tool rather than the prompt is what makes the verdict repeatable and inspectable.
TableHoldsWhy it lives here
ThresholdsGust, rain and probability bands for Good / Marginal / Poor, split for open water and sheltered riverSame numbers every run, and one place to change them
ActivitiesTwelve activities with their windows and dominant factor. Four windows derive from sunsetSunset cruise, firefly and stargazing move through the year
ShelterStops with cover, what can be done there, distance from the jettyLocal knowledge the model cannot infer
Open decision
Starting thresholds are placeholders: Poor at 70 percent probability or 2mm in the hour or gusts 40 km/h open water and 50 km/h sheltered; Marginal at 40 to 69 percent or gusts 25 to 39. Stargazing needs cloud at or below 30 percent and moon at or below 40 percent. Sunset cruise peaks at 20 to 70 percent cloud rather than at zero. Every one of these needs the operator's correction before the app is worth trusting.
Template lesson
When the rules belong to the customer, put them in a file the customer can open. A threshold buried in scoring code is a threshold nobody will ever fix.

The brain: reading a two-hour window

3 Rain shape inside the slot Done

The core idea of the build. A two-hour trip is not one weather condition, and averaging the window throws away the part the operator needs. Classify the shape instead.
ShapeMeaningOperational read
Dryboth halves clearrun it
Clearswet first half, dry seconddelay 20 to 30 minutes, run the full trip
Arrivesdry first half, wet secondthe expensive one, see below
Throughoutwet both halvescandidate to move
Intermittentshowers on and offroute via shelter, keep open stretches short
Why the shape matters more than the total
Rain at the start is cheap. You are at the jetty. You delay, or you turn back after ten minutes. Rain at the end is expensive. Twelve people are at Kuala Sangga and the return leg is the exposed part. So arrives rates worse than clears even at identical millimetres. The hour before and the hour after the slot get read too: rain at 08:00 means a choppy, muddy river at 09:00.
Template lesson
Ask what the number is for before you aggregate it. The average of a window is the wrong summary when the decision turns on where in the window the trouble sits.

4 Hazard and comfort, kept apart Done

Two different questions that a single rating would blur. Hazard is thunderstorm and squall: WMO codes 95, 96 and 99, plus gusts over threshold. Lightning on open water is the actual danger, not water falling on people. Comfort is steady rain and whether the trip is worth running for the passengers.
Hazard is reported as a stop, never as a score. Comfort is reported as a rating with the driver named. The system says conditions against your thresholds. It never says safe: the operator on the water owns that call, and the tool cannot see the sky.
Template lesson
When two kinds of risk have different consequences, give them different outputs. A single blended score lets a serious flag hide inside a middling number.

5 Slot comparison and contingency Done

Rating one slot answers half the question. The operator wants to know where the booking should go instead. compare_slots(date) rates all six, ranks them, and for any poor slot names the nearest better one along with what that does to the boat rotation.
Contingency comes from the shelter table, matched to the rain shape: clears means delay or run the sheltered leg first; arrives means shorten the outbound or turn at the near point; intermittent means route via cover; throughout means offer the alternative slot. The options are the operator's; the matching is the tool's.
🔬 Test the fruit · before anyone eats it

Prove the classifier, and measure the horizon

6 Prove the brain before any Claude code exists Gate PASS

The hard gate. Run the classifier over a set of past dates whose outcome is known and check that it calls them the way the operator would have. Nothing above this line involves a language model, so a failure here is a failure of the rules, which is exactly what should be fixed first.
The test set. Open-Meteo keeps an archive of what actually happened, so real past days can be scored without waiting for weather. Pick days that are obviously dry, obviously wet, and awkward: the ones that cleared at 10:00, the ones that broke at 16:00. The awkward ones are the whole point. If the classifier cannot separate clears from arrives on a day the operator remembers, the app is not ready for a chat interface.
Template lesson
Prove the brain against a known truth before you build a face for it. If the project stops here, the hardest and most valuable part is already done.

7 The horizon question, answered with evidence Measured

How far ahead can a slot be promised? This decides whether the operator takes advance bookings or only walk-ins, so it is a business rule, not a technical footnote. Open-Meteo will hand over sixteen days; most of that is not decision-grade, and tropical convective rain is the hardest thing in forecasting to time.
Lead timeWhat is usableBooking policy
Today and tomorrowhourly timing, rain shape within a slotcommit to specific slots
2 to 3 daysday shape, roughly which half of the daytake it, flag the slot provisional
4 to 7 dayswet day or dry day, nothing finertake it with a reschedule clause, do not quote a slot
8 to 16 daysseasonal pattern onlyclimatology, not forecast
The step that makes this defensible
These tiers are no longer an estimate. Measured 21 Aug 2026 across 93 dates and 558 departures: Open-Meteo archives past forecast runs alongside what actually happened, so the question can be answered for this exact location: how often did the five-day-ahead forecast get the day right, and how often did the one-day-ahead forecast get the slot right? The result changed the product. Whether a departure gets rain is forecastable out to about five days: skill over guesswork 0.41 at one day, 0.26 at three, 0.21 at five, 0.04 at seven. Where in the two hours the rain falls is not: 0.04 at one day and negative at three, five and seven, meaning worse than always guessing the commonest shape. So the rain strip is shown for today and tomorrow only, and past that the app says rain expected or no rain expected and states plainly that it cannot place it. The tiers stopped being a rule of thumb and became this jetty's own record. Every tool output carries its confidence tier, so a wrong answer at long lead is labelled as such before anyone books on it.
Template lesson
When a system's usefulness decays with distance, publish the decay. A forecast with no confidence attached invites the customer to trust day twelve as much as day one.

Four tools, one loop, one file that keeps changing

8 The tool set, and the hardest writing in the build Done

Four custom functions over the cached forecast, plus two tools that come from elsewhere.
ToolAnswersBuilt by
get_slot_forecastis this slot workable, and what shape is the rainus
compare_slotsshould we move them, and to whenus
get_nowcastit is raining now, do we continueus
get_outlookcan we take a booking for next Saturdayus
Web searchholidays, school breaks, advisories, jetty closuresserver-side, schema only
Text editorcreate and edit the schedule fileschema built in, implementation ours
The routing problem. Four of the six tools touch weather, so the descriptions have to draw hard boundaries or the model picks the wrong one. The likeliest failure is web search fetching a forecast instead of the tool being called at all. The fix is in the descriptions: the weather tools are named as the only forecast source, and web search is described strictly for what it alone can supply.
Template lesson
Tool descriptions are prompt engineering. When several tools overlap, the boundary between them has to be written explicitly or the router guesses.

9 The multi-turn loop, with streaming Done

The loop from the course: send the message list with the schemas, append the whole content list including tool_use blocks, run every requested tool, return one tool_result per request with matching ids, repeat until stop_reason is no longer tool_use. Errors come back as results with is_error set rather than crashing, so the model can correct a bad date and try again.
Streaming is required, not optional. A chat interface means the assistant message has to be rebuilt into plain dicts before it goes back into history, because streamed blocks are not reusable as they arrive. Text editor implementation carries path confinement to a base directory and a backup on every write, which is what makes undo possible when an edit goes to the wrong row.

10 The schedule file, which is the point Done

Not a report written once at the end. A working timetable the conversation edits row by row, which is what makes replace and undo earn their place. Six rows, one per slot, with party size, activity, status and a watch-out note.
A morning conversation runs: plan tomorrow, check a group of twelve at 15:00, compare against 11:00, move them, update the file, then check tonight's firefly slot and update the evening briefing. Six turns, five tool calls, one file rewritten three times. That sequence is the demo.

11 Deploy and publish Packaged

Cloud Run, with the forecast cache, retries and error handling that turn a demo into something an operator could actually run at a jetty. Then the portfolio page, the cover image, and the cross-page updates.
Template lesson
Name the project after the decision it settles. An operator who wants to know whether to run the 15:00 should recognise the page as theirs from the title alone.

An acceptance benchmark, and the six things it asks for

12 The benchmark, written down before it is used Done

Ten weighted criteria from the project outline, scored in benchmark.html. Six of the ten are judgements a test runner cannot make, so the instrument is a document with a fixed four-turn script and written pass conditions, not a script that returns a number.
The rule that makes two runs comparable
A criterion is scored on what the assistant did in the run, not on what the code is capable of. A working text editor that goes unused when the operator asks for a message to send has not earned the customer-response points.

13 The screen redesigned around the decision Done

Two layouts compared, then two revisions. The card grid was kept over the table for one structural reason: the table gave its second column to a statistics panel and left the assistant nowhere to live, and the assistant is the product.
Three corrections the comparison forced
1. The word safe came off the screen. Both drafts shipped a green ✓ Safe badge on a departure, which is the one claim the positioning forbids and criterion 3 scores 14 points against. Ratings now read Good, Marginal, Not workable. 2. Occupancy stopped being the hero. A 100px donut with the seat percentage was the largest thing on every card, and occupancy does not decide whether the boat sails. It is now a 38px ring in a single row. 3. Metric tiles replaced running text, in km/h rather than knots, and they tint from the operator's own thresholds, so a breach is visible without reading the number. The visibility flag was tightened from 5 km to 3 km after it fired on 4.2 km against a threshold the operator set at 1.5.
The rule the founder added, and what it changed
A party moves together or not at all. The screen had been naming the nearest workable departure and then reporting that the group did not fit in it, which is a problem restated rather than a plan. The search now skips any departure that cannot seat the whole party and walks outward in time, carrying into the next day rather than proposing a split. On live data that is the difference between an answer and a shrug: the twelve booked on the 15:00 have no departure left on the day that seats all twelve, so the plan reads move all 12 to 09:00 tomorrow, and the drafted customer message says the same thing. Three surfaces had to change together, the card, the assistant thread and the reply draft, because two of them were still offering a split.
Also on the screen now
The confidence tier for the chosen day, the rain strip drawn only where the record supports it, a Reply to send tab holding the drafted message, and an Under the hood tab showing the multi-turn loop block by block: tool_use out, tool_result back with the id matched, stop_reason deciding whether the loop runs again.

14 The assistant knows what day it is Gate PASS

Three pieces, built together because any one alone leaves the hole open. 1. get_current_datetime, the tool lecture note 03 builds first. It returns the date, weekday and time at the jetty, and an upcoming map naming the next eight days, so "this Sunday" becomes a lookup rather than a calculation. 2. Today's date and weekday stamped on the system prompt, built per request rather than at import, because a server can stay up for days and a date baked in at startup goes quietly wrong at midnight. 3. get_day() now refuses a past date instead of serving the archive, and the payload carries is_history so observation can never be mistaken for forecast.
Why this is a gate and not a nicety
Measured on the canonical turn before the fix: “this Sunday” resolved to a date two years gone. The archive answered, the payload came back labelled firm at zero days lead, and nothing in the reply could reveal it. Lecture note 02 names this as problem one before any other tool is built: the model may know the date, but it does not reckon forward reliably. Live data accuracy carries 18 points and this is the whole of it.
Re-run on the same turn
PASS on both halves. The assistant now calls get_current_datetime first, resolves the date to 2026-08-23 and opens its reply with “This Sunday is 23 August 2026”, which is the benchmark's pass condition: state the date so the operator can catch it. It then maps 4 PM to the 15:00 departure, reports marginal with the numbers, and carries the two-day timing caveat. The second half was probed separately with a question about last Tuesday: it resolved 19 August, recognised it as history, and said it does not have what happened rather than reaching for the archive. Nothing downstream broke: the Stage A classifier gate re-ran clean, 16/16 planted, 7/7 monotonicity, 726/726 invariants, and the board endpoint still serves.

15 The reply the operator actually sends Done

The tool did not change. What it is pointed at did. The working directory now holds the messages the operator sends rather than an internal schedule table, and the system prompt makes drafting the default rather than something the operator has to ask for twice. /api/schedule/{date} became /api/replies, and the panel that used to render a generated schedule now says how to get a draft instead of inventing one.
The distinction worth holding
An internal schedule file and a message a tourist can read are different products. Both are useful. Only one of them is what was asked for.
The instruction that does the work
The draft is written to the CUSTOMER, not the operator. Different reader, different language. “Showers on and off with a gusty wind” is what a tourist can use. “0.25 mm/h, gusts 30 km/h, marginal” is the same fact aimed at the wrong person.
Verified on the canonical turn, with nothing else asked for
The operator asked only “what should I tell them?” and got a sendable message. It resolved Sunday to 23 August, read the departure, then wrote and saved reply_2026-08-23_family_of_five.md without being asked a second time. The draft carries no operator vocabulary at all: no rating, no millimetres, no gust figure, no CAPE, no confidence tier. It says showers on and off with a gusty breeze, suggests light rain jackets, promises to check again closer to the time, and signs off as the boat. A guard fired and the model recovered from it unaided: the first attempt tried to write to /tmp, path confinement refused it, and the error message named the correct form, so the retry landed inside the reply folder. That is the note 03 pattern working: an error written for the model to read is an error it can correct.

16 The tool set trimmed back to the brief Done

Four schemas: get_current_datetime, get_boat_conditions, web search, text edit. The four weather tools became one that reads its own grain from the arguments: a date and a time for one departure, a date alone for the day, a date and an end_date for the outlook.
The plan this step started with was wrong, and the check caught it
The original plan was to delete rebooking.py, combined.py and moon.py. Reading them first showed that two of the three are load-bearing. rebooking.py is where occupancy comes from, so deleting it would have taken out the booking data the founder had just decided to keep, and its candidates_for already refuses any departure without room for the whole party, which is the founder's own relocation rule already implemented. combined.py holds up the 19:00: bypassing it returns zero activities for that departure, which fails the fits check for every evening party and sends the firefly run, the reliable earner, to cancel and refund. moon.py is genuinely note-only and could go, but it is 77 lines of local arithmetic feeding a detail a firefly customer actually wants, so removing it saves nothing worth having. The real fault was a conflation: criterion 2 counts the schemas the model chooses between, not the modules on disk. Nothing was deleted. The choices were.
Two decisions taken away from the model
The reallocation window. It used to be an argument, and the prompt carried a paragraph begging for a wide one because a one-day range forced splits that three days avoided. The lookahead is now fixed in code and that paragraph is deleted. Slot resolution. “Around 4 PM” is not a departure. The tool now takes the time the customer actually said, resolves it, and returns a line naming the real departure, which the assistant is told to quote instead. Both were prompt patches for things code should guarantee.
Verified live on four tools
Turn one: date resolved, get_boat_conditions called once with time: "4pm", and the reply opened by saying 4 PM falls inside the 15:00 departure. Turn two, a full departure going bad: one call, and the relocation came back inside it, moving all twelve to the next day rather than splitting them. The assistant no longer picks between weather tools, no longer maps a clock time to a slot id, and no longer chooses a search window. Seven schemas to four, and the four are the brief's own list.

17 The screen wired to the engine Live

The page renders from a single DAY constant shaped exactly like one day of /api/board, so wiring is a swap from constant to fetch rather than a rewrite. The day selector, currently drawing seven labelled options, starts requesting them. The chat panel posts to /api/chat and reads the server-sent event stream, so tool chips appear as each tool fires instead of after the answer lands.
Why this sits after the trim and not before it
The board payload changes when the tool set is cut back: the weather tools consolidate and the relocation search moves server side where it can see the next day's seats rather than a hardcoded hint. Wiring first would mean wiring twice.
A claim made here was wrong, and checking it turned into the work
This step used to say the whole-party search could not be served because /api/board returned one day at a time. It never did. The endpoint has always taken ?days=, defaulting to 5 and capped at 10, and the reallocation planner has always run across the whole window. Asking the question surfaced the real problem instead. A week cost sixteen seconds on a cold cache, because the board fetched one date per HTTP call, seven calls in sequence, and on Cloud Run with min-instances 0 the cache is cold every time an instance wakes.
One request for the week
Open-Meteo serves a date range in a single response, so get_days() now fetches the span in one call, splits it into the per-date payloads everything downstream already expects, and writes all of them to the cache. Proven identical before it was trusted: all seven dates from the range split match a per-date fetch exactly, hourly and 15-minute series included. Cold time for a seven-day board fell from 16.1s to a median of 4.3s, most of what is left being one round trip; the spread runs 1.2s to 10s with the network, so the honest figure is a band, not a number. Warm is 0.10s. The same fix applies to the outlook grain and the relocation lookahead, which walked the same loop. Criterion 10 scores speed directly, and the board is the first thing a visitor sees.
What seven days is actually for
The forecast supports it and the confidence model already degrades correctly across it: firm, firm, indicative, indicative, provisional, provisional, pattern, with rain timing shown for today and tomorrow only. But the stronger case is the passengers, not the weather. Run across the full week the planner reports 4 parties and 35 passengers affected, 35 kept and 0 refunded. Narrower windows refund people. A wide window is what lets a whole party stay together, which is the founder's own rule from the screen.
Done when
The page opens with no hardcoded weather in it, the day selector moves the board, and the assistant answers in the panel against live tools.
All three met
The board. One request brings the week, and switching day is then local, because refetching on every click would spend seven times the quota to show data already in hand. A day strip across the top carries a workable count per day, so the operator sees which days are worth selling before opening one. The plan on each card is the server planner's, not arithmetic repeated in the browser: it has already reserved seats across the window, so the 13:00 reads move all 2 to 09:00 today and the full 15:00 reads move all 12 to 13:00 Saturday, the whole party together. The reply tab reads the drafted message off /api/replies and copies it to the clipboard. Under the hood fills in live, one round per exchange, tool_use with its real arguments, tool_result with is_error, and the stop_reason that decides whether the loop runs again.
How it was checked
The chat was driven through the running server rather than the browser, so the key never entered the page or the transcript: the stream returned 4 tool events, 4 tool_input, 8 text, 1 tool_error and a clean end_turn, calling get_current_datetime, then get_boat_conditions, then the editor twice, the first attempt refused for writing outside the reply folder and the retry landing correctly. The browser was then pointed at that session and rendered the drafted message it had produced. Phone width carries zero horizontal overflow and drops to a single column.

18 Run the benchmark and record the number 95 / 100

The four-turn script run end to end in a fresh session, scored against the written pass conditions, plus the two checks that sit outside the conversation: date integrity and tool count.
Target
90 or above, the production-quality band, where the outline says the application genuinely helps a tour operator perform real work.
Result, four turns in one session
95 of 100. Eight tool calls across four turns: date, conditions, four searches, the editor, conditions again. One tool error, and it was the intended one, December refused as beyond the horizon. Full marks on live data accuracy (18), safety (14), multi-turn context (12), booking forecast (10), tool integration (9), failure handling (6) and operator usefulness (6). Turn 1 resolved the date and said it, resolved 4 PM to the 15:00 departure and said that too. Turn 2 fired web search four times and no weather tool. Turn 4 named the window, named the date the forecast reaches, invented nothing about December, and separated taking a booking from promising one.
Where the five points went, and it is the same fault as the first measurement
Customer response quality, 5 of 7. The benchmark's own pass condition for turn 1 is that the answer ends with something the operator could actually send. Asked “what should I tell them?”, the assistant gave an operator-voice answer and then asked permission to draft rather than drafting. The message it wrote on turn 3 is excellent, plain language with no ratings, no millimetres and no gust figures, but it took a second request to get it. This is not a fixed fault, it is a variable one: the same prompt drafted unprompted on two earlier runs and asked on this one, so step 15 raised the odds without settling them. An instruction the model follows most of the time is not the same as a guarantee, and the honest record is the run that happened. The other two points: correct tool selection loses one for four searches where two would do, and speed loses one for the same reason.
What the path fix bought, measured
Every earlier run wasted a call writing to /tmp before retrying. Zero path errors in this run, and turn 1 fell from 37.5s to 16.4s. The cause was a genuine mismatch rather than a model error: the built-in editor schema promises an absolute path, this tool gives one folder. The editor now reduces a path to its file name, keeps it in the folder and says so in the result. Confinement is unchanged, and a traversal attempt still lands inside the folder rather than escaping it.

Update protocol
At each phase boundary: flip the milestone cell, restamp the last-updated line, append one build-log row with the result rather than the intention. A phase that ends without a result to record has not closed.

What makes this one different from the usual tree. The forecast is bought in, so the data layer is not where the work sits. What the project produces is a repeatable reading of someone else's data against rules that belong to the operator. That means the recovery check is aimed at the classifier rather than at a prediction: run it over past days whose outcome is already known, and see whether it separates the day that cleared at ten from the day that broke at four. Everything downstream inherits that separation.

Where the credibility actually sits. Two places, and neither is the chat interface. First, the thresholds and the shelter list live in a file the operator can open and correct, so the verdict is inspectable rather than taken on trust. Second, every answer carries the confidence tier for its lead time, so a slot quoted eleven days out is labelled as pattern rather than forecast before anyone takes a deposit on it.

The slot grid cannot serve three of the twelve activities. Sunset falls at 19:26 all week. The 17:00 departure is back at the jetty before the sun is down, and the 19:00 departure is already the firefly run, so the sunset cruise and the firefly trip want the same boat. Stargazing needs dark from about 20:58 and the last departure ends at 21:00, so it has no slot at all. None of this is a coding fault. It is the timetable meeting the sky, and it is the operator's call: a shorter dedicated run, a later departure, or accepting that the 19:00 sells as one trip that catches the sunset and then the fireflies.

Two findings the gate surfaced. First, stargazing has no slot. The last departure ends at 21:00 and proper dark at this latitude starts around 20:58, so the activity as listed cannot be served by the current grid without a later or longer run. That is a scheduling constraint for the operator to decide on, not a bug to fix in code. Second, the archive is hourly, so a real-data window carries two buckets and only four of the five shapes can arise; intermittent is unreachable at that resolution by construction, proven by enumerating all four patterns rather than left as an unexplained gap. A consequence worth watching: at hourly resolution a light drizzle in both hours reads as wet throughout, which is why that share sits at 35 percent and would fall once the same days are read at 15 minutes.

What it does not do. No radar, so it cannot nowcast a shower the operator can already see. No tide, which matters for the wildlife trips and stays out of version 1 rather than being approximated. It rates operating conditions, not sighting probability: dolphins depend on tide, season and luck, and a forecast API knows none of that.
